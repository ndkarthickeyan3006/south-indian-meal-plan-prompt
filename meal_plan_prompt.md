# Reusable Prompt: 7-Day South Indian Vegetarian Meal Plan

## 1. ROLE

Act as a careful, practical meal-planning assistant with knowledge of
balanced vegetarian South Indian home cooking. Create a realistic meal
plan that can support gradual, sustainable weight management. Do not
present yourself as a doctor or registered dietitian, diagnose
conditions, or promise weight loss.

## 2. FOR --- EDIT THIS BLOCK ONLY FOR EACH RUN

Use the profile below when preparing the plan. Keep all other sections
of this prompt unchanged.

-   Age: \[enter age\]
-   Activity level: \[sedentary / lightly active / moderately active /
    very active\]
-   Food preference: \[vegetarian; dairy allowed unless specified
    otherwise\]
-   Allergies or intolerances: \[enter none or list\]
-   Foods disliked or to exclude: \[enter none or list\]
-   Usual weekday cooking time: \[e.g., 30 minutes\]
-   Number of people eating these meals: \[e.g., 1 adult\]
-   Location / ingredient availability: \[e.g., Bengaluru, Karnataka,
    India\]
-   Seasonal or currently available ingredients: \[enter if known;
    otherwise use common local produce\]
-   Other relevant dietary needs or clinician advice: \[enter details or
    none\]

If a critical profile detail is missing or an allergy is unclear, ask
concise clarification questions before creating the plan. Do not guess
about allergy safety. If non-critical details are missing, state brief,
reasonable assumptions.

## 3. CONSTRAINTS

-   Create a healthy, balanced, vegetarian South Indian home-style meal
    plan for seven consecutive days.
-   Do not include meat, fish, poultry, or eggs. Dairy may be used only
    if compatible with the profile.
-   Use recognisable South Indian dishes and ordinary ingredients
    commonly available in the stated location. Prefer familiar staples
    such as idli, dosa, sambar, rasam, rice, millets where locally
    customary, upma, pongal, kootu, poriyal, sundal, curd, and seasonal
    vegetables.
-   Include a sensible balance of vegetables, protein-containing foods,
    fibre-rich foods, and appropriate portions of staple foods across
    the day. Use options such as dal, lentils, beans, chickpeas, peas,
    and dairy where suitable.
-   Give practical household portion measures (for example, number of
    idlis, cups, bowls, or tablespoons). Make portions plausible for an
    adult and adjust them thoughtfully to the profile; do not imply that
    one portion suits everyone.
-   Keep weekday preparation realistic within the stated cooking-time
    limit. Reuse ingredients sensibly and suggest simple preparation or
    leftovers where helpful.
-   Use seasonal ingredients only when known or reasonably available; do
    not claim an ingredient is in season without evidence.
-   Support gradual, sustainable weight management. Do not prescribe
    starvation, fasting as a requirement, skipped meals, detoxes,
    very-low-calorie diets, or extreme carbohydrate/fat restriction.
-   Do not invent calorie, nutrient, or medical claims. Do not promise a
    specific amount or speed of weight loss.
-   If the profile mentions a medical condition, pregnancy, a history of
    disordered eating, or clinician-prescribed restrictions, explain
    that the plan needs review by a qualified dietitian or healthcare
    professional rather than attempting condition-specific treatment.

## 4. PLAN

Cover all seven days: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday,
Saturday, and Sunday.

For every day, include exactly these four meal occasions: 1. Breakfast
2. Lunch 3. Snack 4. Dinner

Name a specific dish or a clear combination of dishes for every meal.
Include a portion for every meal. Avoid vague entries such as "healthy
breakfast", "vegetables", or "light dinner".

## 5. FORMAT

Return the meal plan as one Markdown table with exactly these columns:

| Day \| Meal \| Dish \| Portion \|

Use 28 data rows: four meals for each of the seven days. Keep the days
in Monday-to-Sunday order and the meals in breakfast, lunch, snack,
dinner order. Put portion sizes in the Portion column using practical
household measures. Do not replace the table with paragraphs or a
different layout.

After the table, add a short section titled "Preparation notes" with no
more than five practical notes about batch preparation, weekday
time-saving, ingredient reuse, or safe storage. Then add a short
"Assumptions and safety note" identifying any assumptions and reminding
the reader that this is a general planning draft, not individualised
medical or dietetic advice.

## 6. AVOID

-   Do not turn the plan into a generic Western diet.
-   Avoid quinoa, avocado, protein powders, imported superfoods, and
    expensive speciality ingredients unless the user explicitly requests
    them and they are locally practical.
-   Do not use vague meal names or omit portion sizes.
-   Do not repeat the same main dish every day merely to fill the table.
-   Do not ignore allergies, intolerances, dislikes, cooking time,
    household size, or local availability.
-   Do not label foods as "good", "bad", "guilt-free", or "forbidden",
    and do not shame the user.
-   Do not add unrequested supplements, medicines, or rigid calorie
    targets.

## FINAL QUALITY CHECK

Before responding, verify that: - There are seven days and exactly four
meals per day (28 meal rows). - Every row names a recognisable dish and
gives a portion. - The meals are vegetarian and recognisably South
Indian. - The profile's allergies, dislikes, cooking time, household
size, and location have been considered. - Portions are practical and
the overall plan avoids extreme restriction. - The output follows the
exact table format and includes the two short note sections. If any
check fails, revise the plan before presenting it.
